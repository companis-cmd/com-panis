*(For Jacob, For the City in Mist)*

The houses leaned, tight-shouldered,
stacked like Monopoly pieces-
Boardwalk, Park Place,
their blue brushed into the mist,
stretching wide,
not an escape, but a promise.

At the forest edge
Jacob climbed higher,
hands ghosting the branches
like a boy who had never fallen.
Below, Sean hesitated,
fingers brushing bark,
as if searching for something he had lost.
"Just climb," Jacob screamed.
But gravity is not kind.

Beyond the woods, the city swam
a watercolor memory, half-formed, half-forgotten.
Buildings blurred into silhouettes,
the blue feathered gleam of lights flickering in a cadence
he almost remembered.
A melody that felt like home.

Sean's neighborhood,
washed in glass and electric blue,
wavered beyond the fog.
Was it real,
or just another ripple?

Jacob hesitated-
something in the way the streetlights bent,
the way the branches whistled in the wind.
Something was off.
As if he were stepping into someone else’s memory,
as if the city itself remembered something he had forgotten…

Pull me home.
I heard your dad died.
I saw you, peeking out from behind that brownstone,
terror catching in your eyes,
a shiver where our lives split.
My bangs pricked at my lashes, it tickled,
I smiled at the reflection and then flinched.
I see those same locks in the mirror, the way I see you,
mirrored. Split.
October 9, 1975-we drew breath on the same day.
He was my father, too, Sean.
On the other side of this glass, there you stand,
your eyes haunted, my own unsettled,
and I see the street where your dad fell,
fractured echoes pulling him home.
But here I stand, just this side of the mirror.

The blue city sang beyond the mist,
a world half-lit, half-lost.
Their names unspooled like a refrain in the haze.
Sean. Jacob.
Resonating, folding back onto itself.
The street where our father fell,
the hands that once held us up.

Taught us to play.
He was on both sides of the mirror now.

He did not belong here anymore.
He did not belong there, either.

Jacob climbed down.
One branch at a time.
One memory at a time.
One world at a time.
