Years of coaching divers taught me that athletes rehearse the dive before leaving the board and that the body then performs what the mind has already walked through. They also rehearse their dreams, and the dream is set in advance: the gold medal stays the gold medal and any change to it counts as a loss. A child playing make-believe with a handful of action figures runs the same battle again and again too, and the battle is allowed to become a school-house, and then a family, because the child was never serving an ending and is only waiting for the scene to click.

Writing is the child’s game played with the athlete’s rigor, draft after draft of the same chapter with the ending free to change underneath me, so that the gold medal may become a state championship and the state championship a different sport altogether, which is a freedom most athletes are never given.

Make-believe is the thing, and what it becomes, if it ever does, is of no matter. I began The Threshold in the spring of 2022 and I am still editing it. It evolves in my mind and on the page, and into forms I haven’t built yet. It is a “never-ending” story, which is what I dreamed it would be from the start. In a world where reality can change right before our eyes, what is literature but something we can step inside and create while we experience?
